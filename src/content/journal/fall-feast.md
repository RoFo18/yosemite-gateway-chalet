There is something magical about the fall. The hot, hazy days of summer are sliding into memory, and the quiet blanket of winter snow has yet to cover the landscape. Autumn is the forgotten season in between, and it is the perfect time to experience the Sierra and June Lake.

As the mornings get cooler and the days grow shorter, fall is a fine time for quiet reflection. To be still in the Sierra is to sit in wonder. Surrounded by 12,000-foot peaks and the great forests of eastern California, it is a place of contrast, where the sheer scale of the mountains rekindles a childlike sense of wonder.

It is also a place of color: orange and red among the evergreens, against the towering gray stone of the Sierra. Fall color is not the exclusive domain of the eastern seaboard. Up here, the leaves give back the light the sun pours down all summer long. It is a photographer's paradise, and as every amateur photographer knows, a great shot is much easier with a great subject.

Step out of the chalet on a cool fall morning, zip your fleece to the top, and the quiet is the first thing you notice. Beneath the breeze and the whispering treetops, the underbrush rustles as one of a thousand creatures gets ready for winter. With the summer crowds gone home, it is the best time of year to see an elusive marmot, a full and sleepy bear, or a golden eagle.

Standing at the edge of June Lake with the Sierra towering above, you cannot help but feel reconnected to the wild. Whether you come for a new adventure, a personal retreat, or time with the people you love, there are few places on earth so beautiful, inspiring and easy to reach as the Eastern Sierra in the fall.
