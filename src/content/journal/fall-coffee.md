Each year, as the echoes of summer fade and the cool of fall settles across the Sierra, June Lake ignites with autumn colour. As the wildlife prepares for winter, the soundscape takes on a different tenor.

Birdsong, so bright in spring, still welcomes the morning sun, but now carries the mystery of a changing season. Unseen creatures rustle through the underbrush, filling their pantries. And if you listen closely, you will hear fresh coffee brewing, rumbling through the restful slumber of guests like a bear shaking berries from a huckleberry bush.

Sleepy-eyed friends and family soon gather around the breakfast bar like a flock of geese ready to migrate. Over coffee, idle chatter turns to plans for the day: a walk from the Fern Lake trailhead, a stroll along June Lake beach, or photographing the colour from the road behind Gull Lake.

In the fall, every day is as fine as the next for marveling at the high Sierra. For a photographer, light is everything, and there is no better light than the few moments after the sun first clears the peaks around June Lake.

Still, mornings are mornings, and morning people are few. Rolling out of a warm bed is easier only because the quiet of the wilderness makes every hour of sleep feel like two at home in the city. And Ansel Adams country lies just beyond the door.

Every day at June Lake is a chance to connect with friends, to marvel at the Sierra, and to walk in the footsteps of Adams and John Muir, whose experience of this high country left an indelible mark on America. Spend a week at the chalet, and you share in that legacy of adventure.

Of course, there is also something to be said for ignoring the timer on the coffee machine.
