Summer on the June Lake Loop is a water day. Four lakes sit within a few miles of each other, and the creeks that connect them run cold and clear out of the high country. At the chalet, five stand-up paddleboards wait in the garage, so the only question is where to put in.

## Rush Creek above Silver Lake

Our favorite paddle is Rush Creek where it winds into Silver Lake. Put in on Silver Lake and turn upstream. The creek slows as it meanders through the meadow, and the canyon walls rise straight ahead, streaked with snow into early summer. Go in the morning, before the afternoon wind comes up, and you will often have the water to yourself.

## Grant Lake

Grant Lake, the largest lake on the loop, sits at its north end, open and wild. Granite ledges line parts of the shore, the water is cold and clear, and on a hot afternoon it is hard to resist. Check the depth and your landing before anyone jumps.

## Gull Lake and June Lake

Gull Lake, the smallest, is closest to the chalet and home to the rope swing that has launched generations of summers. June Lake has a sandy beach for a slow afternoon. Both are calm in the morning and good for a first paddle with kids.
