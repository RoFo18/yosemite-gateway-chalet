## Where Tioga Pass is

Tioga Pass sits on the crest of the Sierra Nevada between Mount Dana to the east and Gaylor Peak to the west. Highway 120, called Tioga Road inside the park, crosses it on the way from Lee Vining and Highway 395 to Tuolumne Meadows and, eventually, Yosemite Valley. At 9,945 feet it is the highest highway pass in California and in the Sierra Nevada. From the pass the road drops more than 3,000 feet in about a dozen miles to the Mono Basin.

The Tioga Pass entrance station is Yosemite's eastern gate. It is the only way into the park from the east side, which is why the opening of Tioga Road each spring matters so much to everyone on this side of the mountains.

## How the opening works

Tioga Road is not maintained in winter. The National Park Service starts plowing on or about April 15 each year, and clearing usually takes between one and two months. How fast it goes depends on the size of the snowpack and on the April and May weather. The park is clear that the date cannot be predicted, even late in the spring.

In practice the road most often opens in late May or June. A few recent reference points:

| Year | Tioga Road opened | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | May 2 | Light snow year; the earliest opening since 1987 |
| 2023 | July 1 | Record snowpack after a historic winter |
| 2026 | May 15 | An early opening |

The National Park Service publishes the full year-by-year record of opening and closing dates, with the April 1 snowpack for each year, on its historical dates page. Check it, and the current conditions page, before you plan around the pass.

## When it closes

The road closes for the season when real snow arrives, usually in November and occasionally in late October. Early storms can also shut it for a few days and then let it reopen. Once it closes for the winter, there is no way to drive into Yosemite from the east side. Read our guide to [Yosemite's east side in winter](/guides/yosemite-east-side-in-winter/) for what that means.

## Driving to Tioga Pass from June Lake

From the Yosemite Gateway Chalet, follow Highway 158 out of the June Lake Loop to Highway 395, drive north to Lee Vining, and turn west on Highway 120 at the Whoa Nellie Deli. The climb up Lee Vining Canyon is about 12 miles of steep, beautiful road past Ellery and Tioga Lakes to the entrance station. Allow about 40 minutes, and fill the tank in Lee Vining, since there is no fuel inside the park on this side.

## Hikes at the pass

{{ figure("granite-dome-and-pond", "A granite dome reflected in a small high country pond", None, "right") }}

### Gaylor Lakes

The classic short hike, starting right at the entrance station. It runs 2.6 miles round trip, with about 500 feet of steep climbing at the start, to a basin of high lakes with views across to the Cathedral Range. Allow two to three hours. Above the upper lake you will find the ruins of the Great Sierra Mine.

### Mono Pass

From the Dana Meadows trailhead a few miles inside the park, an 8-mile round trip with about 1,000 feet of gain leads to Mono Pass and the old cabins beyond it. Allow five to seven hours. Strong hikers can continue down Bloody Canyon toward Walker Lake and the June Lake side, the old route between Yosemite and the Mono Basin.

### Mount Dana

At 13,061 feet, Mount Dana is the second-highest peak in Yosemite and one of the most accessible big summits in the Sierra. The route climbs steeply from near the entrance station on a use trail. It is a serious day at high altitude: start early, carry plenty of water, and turn around if afternoon thunderstorms build.

### Tioga Peak and Gardisky Lake

Just outside the park, a short drive up Saddlebag Lake Road, a steep trail climbs about 0.7 miles to Gardisky Lake. From there, an easy scramble up the northwest slope reaches the rounded summit of Tioga Peak, which has arguably the best view of the whole Tioga Pass region.

## Spring skiing at Tioga Pass

For a few weeks after the road opens, Tioga Pass is one of the best spring skiing destinations in North America. Snow lingers on the north faces of Gaylor Peak, Mount Dana and the peaks around Ellery Lake, and skiers come from across the country for corn snow under clear skies. This is avalanche terrain. Go with a guide, or with full avalanche training and equipment.

## A little history

The road began as the Great Sierra Wagon Road, built in 1883 to serve the Great Sierra Mine near the pass. The silver never paid, and in 1915 Stephen Mather, soon to be the first director of the National Park Service, helped buy the road and give it to the government. The pass takes its name from the Tioga Mine.

## Before you go

- **Fees:** an entrance fee applies, and a new fee structure for nonresident visitors took effect in 2026. Check the National Park Service fees page.
- **Reservations:** none were required to enter Yosemite in 2026. Confirm for your year.
- **Altitude:** the pass is almost 10,000 feet. Drink water, go slowly, and expect cold mornings even in August.
- **Weather:** summer afternoons often bring thunderstorms. Be off summits by early afternoon.
- **Road status:** the National Park Service conditions page and Caltrans QuickMap have the latest.

## Where to stay near Tioga Pass

The closest beds are in Lee Vining, about 12 miles from the entrance. June Lake, 27 miles away, puts you among four lakes, a ski mountain and trails from the door. Mammoth Lakes, a little farther, has the most choice. Read our full guide to [lodging near Yosemite's east entrance](/yosemite-national-park-lodging/).
