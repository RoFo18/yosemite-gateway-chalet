## The June Lake Loop

Highway 158 leaves US 395 south of Lee Vining, curves west into a glacial valley below Carson Peak, and returns to 395 farther north, a horseshoe of about 14 miles. Along the way it passes June Lake and its village, Gull Lake, June Mountain, Silver Lake, Rush Creek and Grant Lake, with steep granite walls on one side and the Ansel Adams Wilderness beyond. People have called it the Switzerland of California for generations.

The Yosemite Gateway Chalet sits on the loop one mile past June Mountain, between the ski area and the Double Eagle Resort, about a mile up-canyon from the Fern Lake trailhead.

## The four lakes

{{ figure("june-lake-loop-dock-morning", "A small aluminum rental boat tied to a wooden dock on a mirror-still lake, with morning mist and a snow-dusted granite peak reflected", "Morning on the loop, before anyone else is up.") }}

**June Lake** is the one that gives the town its name, with a sandy beach, a marina and the village on its shore. **Gull Lake**, the smallest, is tucked into the village and has the calmest water for paddling. **Silver Lake** sits deep in the Loop under the cliffs, with phenomenal sunrises and some of the best trout water on the loop. **Grant Lake**, the largest, is a reservoir at the north end, open and windswept, with good bait fishing where the creek comes in. Our [fishing guide](/fly-fishing/) has the details.

## June Mountain

June Mountain is a small, friendly ski area right on the loop, with about 2,580 vertical feet of terrain and far fewer crowds than its sister resort in Mammoth. It is also known for excellent lift-assisted backcountry skiing. See [winter at June Lake](/experiences/winter/).

## The seasons

**Winter** brings deep snow, skiing at June Mountain and Mammoth, snowshoeing and ice climbing. The village is quiet and Tioga Road is closed.

**Spring** is snowmelt season. The fishing opener draws anglers from across California, and when [Tioga Road opens](/guides/tioga-pass-opening-dates/) the high country of Yosemite returns.

**Summer** is for hiking, paddling, fishing, climbing and day trips over Tioga Pass to Tuolumne Meadows. Afternoons can bring thunderstorms.

**Fall** is the secret. The aspens on the loop turn gold, usually in October, the crowds thin out, and the light is the best of the year. See our [fall colors guide](/guides/june-lake-fall-colors/).

## A week from June Lake

1. **Settle in.** Walk out the back door, a mile down the Loop and up the trail to Fern Lake, then tacos at La Parrilla in the village.
2. **Tuolumne Meadows.** Over Tioga Pass early for Cathedral Lakes or Lembert Dome. See [Tuolumne Meadows](/guides/tuolumne-meadows/).
3. **The lakes.** Paddle Gull Lake, fish Silver Lake at sunrise, swim from the beach at June Lake.
4. **Mono Lake and Bodie.** The tufa towers at dawn and the ghost town after lunch. See [sightseeing](/experiences/sightseeing/).
5. **Tioga Pass.** Gaylor Lakes, or a long day up Mount Dana. See [Tioga Pass](/guides/tioga-pass/).
6. **Mammoth.** The gondola, Devils Postpile, or the hot springs.
7. **Rest.** A long morning, the spa, and the hydrotherapy tub at sunset.

## Getting to June Lake

June Lake is about five hours from Los Angeles on US 395 and under six from the Bay Area in summer, when Tioga Pass is open. Reno is about 155 miles north. Mammoth Yosemite Airport is about 25 minutes south, with seasonal service. In summer, the YARTS bus links June Lake with Tuolumne Meadows and Yosemite Valley. See [directions and distances](/location/).

## Essentials in the village

The June Lake General Store has groceries, hardware, liquor and camping supplies. Ernie's Tackle and Ski Shop is the place for fishing advice and snowshoe rentals. Many businesses keep seasonal hours, so call ahead in spring and late fall.

## Where to stay in June Lake

June Lake has small resorts, cabins and motels near the lakes, plus private vacation homes. Read our guide to [June Lake lodging](/june-lake-lodging/).
