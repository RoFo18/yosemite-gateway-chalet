- group: Will it work for us?
  q: How many people does the chalet sleep?
  a: Up to eight overnight guests, and infants count. No more than ten people may be on the property at any time. Mono County sets these limits for fire safety, our license depends on them, and we enforce them. Rates cover six; a seventh and eighth guest are $59 a night each, waived for active-duty military families.
- q: What are the beds, and where is everyone sleeping?
  a: 'Three suites upstairs, each with its own bath: the master suite has a California King and a loft above it with a pull-out sofa; the Elsie Room has a California King; the River Room has a queen. Downstairs, a pull-out futon in the family room, off the kitchen, sleeps one or two more. All the beds are memory foam. The floorplan is the next question down.'
- q: What is the floorplan?
  a: 'Two floors. Downstairs: the Grand Room, the kitchen, the dining room, the family room with the pull-out futon, the mud room and the back deck with the hot tub. Upstairs: three suites, each with its own bath. Both plans are below.'

- q: Do we get the whole chalet?
  a: Yes. All 4,000 square feet are yours for the stay, and nobody else is down the hall. We do not rent individual rooms.
- q: How private is the loft in the master suite?
  a: Like any loft, whoever sleeps there has a commanding view of the room below. It is open to the master bedroom below, so it suits children or close friends best.
- q: Are there stairs?
  a: Yes. Plan on walking up steps from the driveway to the front door with your bags, and the three suites are upstairs, reached by a staircase from the Grand Room. Pack like you will be carrying it yourself, because you will. In winter, wear boots for the walk in.

- q: Is the chalet good for kids?
  a: 'Very. Kids 12 and under ski free all season at June Mountain, a mile away. There is a kid-size paddleboard alongside the five full-size boards, a climbing wall in the garage for stormy afternoons, and Yost Meadow is a walk from the back door. They will sleep like somebody paid them to. One rule: guests under 18 may not use the hot tub.'
- q: Do you have a crib or a pack and play?
  a: Yes. A pack and play lives in the River Room closet, so you can leave yours at home and use the trunk space for ski boots. Infants count toward the eight-guest limit.
- q: Are pets allowed?
  a: No pets, anywhere on the property. Any animal, whether a service animal, an emotional support animal or anything else, must be approved in advance with real paperwork from a licensed physician (MD or DO) who treats you. Certificates bought online do not count. People with real disabilities depend on these accommodations, and we take that seriously.
- group: What it costs, and booking
  q: How much does the chalet cost per night?
  a: From $295 a night on quiet midweek dates to $995 at the Christmas peak, for up to six guests. Book direct through our availability calendar, or see live prices on VRBO and Airbnb.
- q: Are there taxes or additional charges?
  a: Three, and no others. $59 a night each for a seventh and eighth guest, infants included (waived for active-duty military families); a flat $295 cleaning fee per stay; and Mono County's 15% transient occupancy tax. All of it is in your quote before you book.
- q: Do you offer a military discount?
  a: Yes. For active-duty military families, we waive the $59 nightly fee for a seventh and eighth guest. Tell us when you inquire. Thank you for your service. We mean that.
- q: How do we book?
  a: 'Book direct: check our availability calendar on the Inquire page and send us your dates, and we will reply with an exact quote and no platform service fee. Or book instantly on VRBO (listing 452799) or Airbnb. Same chalet, same family, either way.'
- q: What is your cancellation policy?
  a: 'For direct bookings: cancel more than 30 days before arrival and we refund your deposit less a $150 administration fee, and each change of dates carries the same $150 fee. Within 30 days, there is no refund unless another party books your dates. Bookings on VRBO and Airbnb follow the policy shown on the listing. The details are on [rates and policies](/rates-and-policies/).'
- q: What happens if there is a wildfire?
  a: If wildfire or heavy smoke makes a stay unsafe or the area is closed, we will work with you to move your dates. Your payment becomes a credit toward another stay of like value within one year. We do not offer cash refunds for fire, storms or other acts of nature, because we do not control them either. Be reasonable with us, and we will be more than reasonable with you.
- group: Getting here, and the weather
  q: How far is the chalet from Yosemite?
  a: 27 highway miles to Yosemite's Tioga Pass entrance, about 40 minutes, and about 45 minutes to Tuolumne Meadows. Yosemite Valley is about 2 to 2.5 hours away by Tioga Road. Tioga Road is open seasonally, usually from late May or June to about November; see [when Tioga Pass opens](/guides/tioga-pass-opening-dates/) for the full record.
- q: I'm coming to June Lake from the Bay Area. When does Tioga Pass close?
  a: 'Usually mid-November, with the first real storm after about November 1. Since 1980 the median closing is November 13, and half of all years close between November 9 and November 19. The earliest was October 17 (2004), the latest January 17 (the 2011 season, a dry fall that just would not quit). Here is the part most people get wrong: October storms almost never close it for good. In 46 falls, none of the storms in the first half of October did, and only 3 of 21 in the second half. Crews plow it, the sun melts the rest, and the road reopens. After November 1, the same storm usually ends the season, and these days the park often closes the road the evening before the snow arrives, not after. Big-snow springs tend to run about ten days later in the fall; dry ones close right on schedule. Check the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) and [Caltrans QuickMap](https://quickmap.dot.ca.gov/) before you leave, not after you are sitting at the gate. And if it is closed, you can still get here. Sonora Pass (Highway 108) and Monitor Pass (Highway 89) usually hang on a few weeks longer than Tioga, though they close for winter too. Highway 88 over Carson Pass, US 50 over Echo Summit and down Kingsbury Grade, and I-80 over Donner stay open all winter, chain controls permitting, and all three drop you onto US 395 heading south to the Loop. Figure an hour or two longer than the Tioga route. The road through Yosemite is prettier. The road around it still ends at the hot tub.'
- q: Can we fly into Mammoth?
  a: Yes. Mammoth Yosemite Airport is about 25 minutes from the chalet, with seasonal commercial service and rental cars. In winter, a storm can delay connections. The mountains do not check your itinerary.
- q: Will we get snowed in?
  a: Not by us. The chalet sits right on Highway 158, not up a side road, and we hire professional plowing for the driveway, so you are plowed out first and on the June Mountain lift while guests up the back roads are still waiting. What we cannot plow is the highway. In a major storm, Caltrans can close Highway 395 and parts of 158, and chain controls are common. That is weather, not us. We will work with you as best we can, but we do not control the road, so carry chains and check Caltrans QuickMap before you drive.
- q: Do we need to worry about bears?
  a: 'Bears, yes. People, hardly at all. Like most rural towns, June Lake has very little crime. The bears are the bigger risk to your car, by a mile. Take every bit of food, trash and anything that smells good out of the car the moment you arrive, and do not leave it in the car overnight. Pro tip: leave the car unlocked. A black bear knows how to work a door handle. If the door opens, he climbs in, sniffs around, decides you are boring and leaves. He will not close the door behind him, because nobody raised him right, but he will not tear the car apart either. Lock it, and he comes in through the window.'

- group: In the house
  q: What are check-in and check-out times?
  a: Check-in begins at 4:30 pm. Check-out is at 10:00 am. Nobody likes that second number, us included.
- q: Is the hot tub open year round?
  a: Yes. The outdoor hydrotherapy tub runs all year, in summer under the stars and in winter with snow banked around it. Guests under 18, pregnant guests, and anyone with a heart condition or pacemaker may not use it.
- q: Is there air conditioning?
  a: Yes. The chalet has air conditioning, so an August afternoon in the Sierra stays comfortable inside. In winter, geothermal heat pumps keep the house warm when the nights drop well below zero.
- q: Is there wifi?
  a: Yes, throughout the house. Strong enough for the one person who swore they would not check work email.
- q: Do we need to bring linens or towels?
  a: No. The house is equipped with organic cotton sheets and towels for the beds, the baths and the tubs.
- q: What should we bring for the kitchen?
  a: Food, your spices, and your coffee. The kitchen is stocked down to the coffee maker, the grinder and the garlic press, and we leave salt and olive oil, but bring your own spices, your coffee and a pack of coffee filters to be safe. And no, there is no pod machine. This is a log cabin, not a dentist's waiting room. Somebody always brings a good knife from home anyway. You will not need it.
- q: Is there a washer and dryer?
  a: Yes, a high-end washer and dryer in the garage. Bring your own laundry detergent. We try to keep some in stock, but we cannot promise it, and the general store does not always have it either.
- q: Where are the extra paper towels and toilet paper?
  a: In the hidden closet on the left on your way into the dining room. Pull the wooden strip to open it. The vacuum and the mop live there too, for the day somebody's hot cocoa ends up on the floor.
- q: Do we bring firewood?
  a: No need to start with any. A small batch of firewood will be waiting as a welcome, enough to get the first fire going and settle the argument about who builds it. For a longer stay, bring more or pick some up in the village.
- q: Can we use a barbecue?
  a: Because of high fire danger in the Eastern Sierra, we discourage barbecues. Smokey Bear has opinions, and so do we. If you must, you may bring a propane barbecue for the back deck.
- group: Out the door
  q: Do kids ski free at June Mountain?
  a: Yes. Kids 12 and under ski free all season at June Mountain with a no-cost child season pass, reserved on IkonPass.com or issued at the ticket office. The mountain is a mile from the chalet.
- q: Are there paddleboards?
  a: Yes. Five stand-up paddleboards and a kid-size board in the garage are included with your stay. Silver Lake and Rush Creek are a few minutes up the road. Go early. Silver Lake is glass in the morning, and by two o'clock the wind comes up and the paddle gets more educational.
- q: Can I really ski from the top of June Mountain to the hot tub?
  a: Yes, with a local guide service. June Mountain is known for some of the best lift-assisted backcountry skiing in North America, and a guided run can end at the chalet's hydrotherapy tub.
- q: Can I hike from the back door into Yosemite?
  a: 'Yes, but only if you are in real shape and know what you are doing: long miles, high passes, no cell service and fast-changing weather. Our advice is to do it in reverse. Ride the YARTS bus up to Tuolumne Meadows in summer and hike back to June Lake. You get more downhill, and there are several routes, from Mono Pass down Bloody Canyon, to the high route over Koip Peak Pass past the Alger Lakes and down to Gem Lake, Agnew Lake and Silver Lake. Arrange your pickup or car shuttle before you go.'
- group: House rules
  q: Can we have a small wedding at the chalet?
  a: 'Technically, yes. If the whole thing, bride, groom, officiant, photographer and the aunt who cries at everything, fits inside eight overnight guests, ten people on the property and three cars, you are welcome to say your vows under Carson Peak. In practice, nobody wants a wedding that small, and small weddings always grow. So the honest answer is no: no weddings, parties or reunions. The limits come from Mono County, our business license depends on them, and we enforce them without exceptions.'
- q: Why is there a three-car limit?
  a: The June Lake Loop is quiet, and our neighbors matter to us. No more than three cars at any time, and that includes a fourth car dropping someone off. If it is not one of your three, it does not pull in.
- q: Can we park in the garage?
  a: No. The garage holds the climbing wall, the paddleboards and the washer and dryer, so there is no room for a car. Park in the driveway, three cars at most, and keep clear of the roofline in winter. The roof sheds snow, and it does not check whose car is underneath.
- group: If we are full
  q: Where do you recommend if you are full?
  a: 'Honestly? Book earlier. There may be bigger houses in June Lake, but this is the nicest chalet you can rent here, and it is a commercially zoned lodging property, licensed and inspected, owned and run by the same family that answers your inquiry. A lot of vacation rentals are run by property managers who are thinking about powder and fishing before they are thinking about you. We own it and we manage it. That is the difference. If our dates are gone, here is where we send people: [Silver Lake Resort](https://www.silverlakeresort.net/store-cafe/), with classic cabins on the lake and one of the best breakfasts on the Loop. It is seasonal, and some cabin weeks have been booked by the same families for generations, so a summer week there opens up about as often as somebody writes it into a will. Next, [June Lake Pines Cottages](https://www.junelakepines.com/) in the village, good value and well run for years. And the [Double Eagle Resort](https://doubleeagle.com/), a mile from the chalet, with a spa, a gym, a pool and a fishing pond for little kids. Then book us for next year, before somebody else does.'
