## The Creekside Spa

The Double Eagle's Creekside Spa is about a mile from the chalet, and you can walk there on the Forest Service trail through a grove of aspens. Get the massage, the workout and the swim, and walk home loose. It offers dozens of treatments for the end of a long day outdoors, and treatment guests can use the fitness center and indoor pool. [Double Eagle Resort and Spa](https://www.doubleeagle.com/).

## Wild hot springs: we go north

A lot gets written about the hot springs around Mammoth, and they are lovely. They also come with more people, more parking trouble and more folks filming themselves for the internet. We are fans of the two north of June Lake, near Bridgeport, on the way to or from Yosemite. Both are free, public, natural and clothing optional.

### Travertine Hot Springs

A string of pools on white and yellow mineral terraces just southeast of Bridgeport, with big views of the Sierra crest. It is a short walk to the main pools, and the quieter side pools are a little farther on.

### Buckeye Hot Springs

Creekside pools in the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest west of Bridgeport, with a small hot waterfall coming off a travertine hillside. The hot water mixes with cold Buckeye Creek, so you can usually find your temperature by moving a few feet.

### Doing both in a day

Plenty of people do: Travertine first for the easy access, then Buckeye for the creek. Go on a weekday if you can, pack out everything you bring in, check the roads after rain or snow, and expect little or no cell service.

{{ note("Hot springs are natural and unregulated. Water temperatures vary and can be dangerous, especially at Hot Creek itself, where soaking is prohibited. Follow posted rules.") }}
