Every hike on this page starts within half an hour of the chalet, and the best of them start at the back door. For trails inside Yosemite, see our [Tuolumne Meadows guide](/guides/tuolumne-meadows/), and before you plan on Tioga Road, read [when Tioga Pass actually opens](/guides/tioga-pass-opening-dates/).

## Fern and Yost Lakes, from the chalet

Leave the car at the house. Walk out the back door and about a mile down-canyon to the Fern Lake trailhead, then up to Fern Lake, a small lake in a cirque on the southern shoulder of Carson Peak. About a mile up, a branch splits off toward Yost Lake and Yost Meadow, a longer walk that many guests say rivals Tuolumne Meadows. Yost Creek is the same water that runs past the hot tub, so you can follow it home.

Carry water, start early in summer, and turn around if thunderheads build over the crest in the afternoon. They do not care that you are almost there.

## Carson Peak, from the back door

You can climb Carson Peak, the mountain you see from the hot tub, without getting in a car. Take the Fern Lake trail, continue past the lake up the east slope to the summit plateau, then walk north to the top. Few rentals anywhere can offer a 10,000-foot summit from the back door.

{{ trail_card("Carson Peak", [("Start", "The back door"), ("Summit", "10,909 ft"), ("Climb", "About 3,300 ft"), ("Time", "A long, full day"), ("Way down", "Scree run, then Rush Creek to Silver Lake")], img="carson-peak-summit-view", alt="The view west from the summit of Carson Peak across the high Sierra, with jagged snow-streaked peaks and an alpine lake", cap="From the top. You walked here from the chalet, which is something you get to bring up at dinner for the rest of your life.") }}

The fast way down is the scree run on the back side: a long chute of loose gravel you descend in big sliding steps, like a sand dune made of mountain. Our kids did it when they were small. It is not for everybody, and it is no place for bad knees or new boots, but it is the most fun you will have going downhill without skis. It drops you toward Agnew Lake, and the Rush Creek trail takes you down to Silver Lake. Arrange a pickup there, or walk the last couple of miles back along the loop.

## Into the backcountry from Silver Lake

The Rush Creek trailhead at Silver Lake, five minutes up the road, is the front door to the Ansel Adams Wilderness: Agnew Lake, Gem Lake and, for backpackers, Waugh Lake, Thousand Island Lake and the John Muir Trail. Morning shade, big granite, and the sound of Rush Creek the whole way up.

## Reversed Peak

{{ figure("family-hike-toward-reversed-peak", "Two small children with backpacks following an adult with a big pack along a sunny trail through sage and pines toward granite peaks", "Backpacking toward Reversed Peak. You are still inside the June Lake Loop.") }}

The trailhead is on North Shore Drive, northeast of Gull Lake, which means you never leave the June Lake Loop, and yet by the first switchback it feels like you have. It is a good first overnight for small backpackers: isolated, beautiful, and close enough to bail to a hot tub if somebody mutinies. The Snow Lakes, small basins that hold water in spring, are the highlight, and the trail continues past Aerie Crag to the summit.

## Parker Lake

{{ trail_card("Parker Lake", [("Trailhead", "About 8,000 ft"), ("Round trip", "About 4 miles"), ("Climb", "About 550 ft"), ("Option", "Parker Bench, up to 10 miles round trip")], img="parker-bench-silver-lake-view", alt="Hikers with packs on the Parker Bench trail, with Silver Lake far below in the June Lake canyon", cap="On the Parker Bench option, looking down to Silver Lake. The best first day at altitude on the loop.") }}

About half a mile past Grant Lake heading toward Highway 395, turn left onto the dirt road signed for Parker Lake and follow signs to the trailhead. Past the first steep pitch, the trail levels out beside Parker Creek through meadow and aspen, and ends at a deep lake under the walls of the Sierra crest.

{{ figure("parker-lake-trail-fall-color", "Gold and orange aspen groves on the slopes below a dark snow-dusted peak, on the way to Parker Lake", "The walk in to Parker Lake in October. Go then if you can.") }}

**The Parker Bench option.** At the junction signed for Silver Lake, turn left instead of continuing to Parker Lake. The trail climbs onto Parker Bench, a high sagebrush shoulder with long views down the canyon to Silver Lake and out to Mono Lake. It is spectacular in fall, when the aspens below turn.

## Glass Creek Meadow

About twenty minutes south of the chalet, off Highway 395 near Obsidian Dome, the Glass Creek trail climbs beside the creek into a broad meadow under the San Joaquin Ridge, in the Owens River Headwaters Wilderness. In early summer the corn lilies come up in thick green stands and the wildflowers follow.

## Horseback riding from the pack station

Some of the best days our guests have here start a few minutes down the road at [Frontier Pack Train](https://www.monocounty.org/listing/frontier-pack-train/1103/), the June Lake Loop's own pack station near Silver Lake. Real wranglers, sure-footed horses matched to every rider from first-timer to expert, and trails that climb straight out of the canyon to views most visitors never see. In fall, they run a multi-day horse drive from June Lake to Bishop.

## Gibbs Lake and Kidney Lake

On Highway 395 south of Lee Vining, turn west on the dirt road signed for Horse Meadows. Most cars will need to park about a mile before the trailhead. Follow the steep old road through the forest to its end, then the trail to Gibbs Lake. Kidney Lake is another mile and almost a thousand feet higher.

## Lee Vining Creek Trail

An easy walk from the south end of Lee Vining, along a creekside forest coming back to life after decades of water diversion. It ends at the Mono Basin Scenic Area Visitor Center above Mono Lake.

{{ trail_card("Lee Vining Creek Trail", [("Distance", "About 1.5 miles each way"), ("Difficulty", "Easy"), ("Ends at", "Mono Basin Visitor Center")], cap="The one for tired legs, grandparents and the morning after Carson Peak.") }}

## Tioga Peak

A rounded summit just outside Yosemite's Tioga Pass boundary, with arguably the best view of the whole Tioga Pass region. Park across from the Gardisky Lake trailhead on Saddlebag Lake Road, climb to Gardisky Lake, then take the northwest slope to the top. Only when [Tioga Road is open](/guides/tioga-pass-opening-dates/). More in the [Tioga Pass guide](/guides/tioga-pass/#tioga-peak-and-gardisky-lake).

## Lundy Canyon

From Highway 395 north of Lee Vining, drive past Lundy Lake to the end of the dirt road beyond the beaver ponds. The trail climbs through aspen groves, wildflowers, cliffs and waterfalls.

{{ trail_card("Lundy Canyon", [("Distance", "As far as you like"), ("Highlights", "Beaver ponds, waterfalls, aspen"), ("Best", "Late September and October")], cap="When the aspens turn, this is one of the finest walks in the Sierra, and the beavers will act like they did it themselves.") }}

## Tuolumne Grove of Giant Sequoias

About 74 miles west on Tioga Road near Crane Flat, and worth the stop if you are driving on to the Bay Area. The grove holds a couple of dozen mature giant sequoias. The paved trail drops about 400 feet to the grove, so the work comes on the way back.
