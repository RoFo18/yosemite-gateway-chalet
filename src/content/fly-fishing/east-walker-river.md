## The river

Bridgeport Reservoir releases cold, food-rich water into a short, fast, slippery stretch of river that grows browns and rainbows out of proportion to its size. Fish average around 15 inches, twenty-inchers are not news, and the river's records are a 13-pound brown and a 9-pound rainbow. You will catch more rainbows. You will remember the browns.

## Read the flow before anything else

The irrigation district controls the release, and the release controls your day. Check the flow before you leave the chalet.

{{ guide_says("I did not check the flow once. It was a beautiful drive to Bridgeport. The burger was excellent. The river was not fishable. Check the flow.") }}



| Flow | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under 75 cfs | Low and clear; fish spook, nymph the deeper runs, think about the temperature |
| 75 to 250 cfs, steady | The sweet spot |
| 250 to 350 cfs | Fishable, harder wading, streamers come into their own |
| Over 350 cfs | The Miracle Mile's slick rocks become genuinely dangerous |

In a low-snow midsummer, fish the early morning only and stop when the water passes 68°F.

## Through the season

| Season | Hatches | Flies |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Skwala stones, baetis, golden stones | Skwala 10, pheasant tail 16 to 18, golden stone nymph 6 to 8 |
| Early summer | PMDs from May, caddis evenings | PMD sparkle dun 16, elk hair caddis 12 to 18, green rock worm |
| Late summer to fall | Terrestrials, caddis, browns chasing | Hoppers, foam beetles, Chernobyl ants, sculpins, crayfish |

## Where

The Miracle Mile, from the dam to the Highway 182 bridge, is the famous water and fishes like it. The canyon below 182 sees a fraction of the people and holds plenty of fish over 20 inches. Across the state line, Nevada manages more public water on the Rosaschi Ranch section, catch and release with barbless flies or lures; you will need a Nevada license there.

## The one thing

Weight. Big browns live on the bottom of the deep runs. If you are not ticking rocks now and then, you are fishing above them.
