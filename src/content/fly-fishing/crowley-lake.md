## The lake

Crowley is the reservoir at the bottom of the Long Valley Caldera, fed by the Owens and a handful of cold creeks. It is shallow, weedy and absurdly productive, and California plants hundreds of thousands of trout here each year to join the ones that grew up wild. The browns get large. The cutthroat are Lahontans. Everybody eats midges.

## The midge game

The Crowley method is not complicated, which is why most people get it wrong. Three chironomid pupae, spaced on the leader, under an indicator, at exactly the depth the fish are holding. Then you wait. Then you adjust the depth a foot. Then you wait again. The take is a twitch of the indicator, not a plunge. The local Mammoth shops call this a technique. Your spouse will call it a nap.

## Through the season

| Months | What is happening | Flies |
|---|---|---|
| Late April to May | Opener crowds, midges, callibaetis nymphs | Chironomids 16 to 18, callibaetis nymph 16 |
| June and July | Damselflies around the weed beds, fish moving to the cold inflows | Damsel nymphs 10 on an intermediate line, chironomids |
| Late July to September | Trout herding baby perch in the shallows | Perch fry patterns 4, olive buggers, tui chub imitations |
| October to Nov. 15 | Cooler water, fewer anglers, big browns hunting | Perch patterns, sculpins, chironomids in the deep bays |

## Where on the lake

McGee Bay is the summer spot, near the cold creek water, and for a few days each July fish stack up in the channels there. Hilton Bay, Sandy Point and Green Banks suit float tubes and small boats. Layton Springs fishes well in spring and late summer. The Owens inlet is the biggest cold-water source on the lake and is choked with weeds by midsummer, so a float tube helps.

Crowley Lake Fish Camp at the south end has the launch and the rentals. The afternoon wind is reliable and serious; be off the open water before it arrives.

## The one thing

Depth. If your indicator is not moving, your flies are at the wrong depth, not the wrong color. Change the depth first.
