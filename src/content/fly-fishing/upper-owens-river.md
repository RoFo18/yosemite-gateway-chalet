## The river

Big Springs pours out of the ground just east of Highway 395, and from there the Owens snakes through a wide sagebrush meadow toward Crowley Lake. Undercut banks, deep bends, weed lines and grass that hangs over the water: every bend is a hiding place, and every hiding place has a fish under it. Resident browns and rainbows live here all year.

Then twice a year the lake comes to visit.

## The runs

In spring, rainbows and cutthroat push up from Crowley to spawn. In fall, fish follow them up to eat their eggs, and the browns get territorial. For a few weeks each season a meadow stream holds fish of 18 to 20 inches and more. You will see them. They will see you first.

{{ guide_says("A lake-run rainbow in a creek this small is a linebacker in a phone booth. Land it fast, release it faster, and celebrate with a domestic lager on the tailgate, as nature intended.") }}



- **Approach like you owe the fish money.** On the Owens, you probably do. Stay low and back from the bank; your shadow ends the conversation.
- **Fish the outside bends and undercuts,** dead-drifting a nymph or swinging a bugger right under the grass.
- **Small eggs and dark buggers in spring and fall,** small nymphs and dries when the fish are looking up.

## Through the year

| Season | What is happening | Flies |
|---|---|---|
| March to May | Lake-run fish, BWOs and midges on cloudy days | Parachute BWO 16 to 20, RS2, zebra midge, flashback pheasant tail, eggs |
| Early summer | PMDs and caddis build | PMD 16 to 18, elk hair caddis 14 to 16 |
| July and August | Trico mornings on the flats, caddis, hoppers on windy days; the water warms | Trico 20 to 22, hopper 10, gold-bead hare's ear 14 to 16 |
| September to November | BWOs return, lake-run fish, browns on the move | BWO 18, pheasant tail 16, eggs, sculpins |
| Winter | Lake-run fish hold deep | Eggs, squirmies, dark streamers fished slow |

The spring water keeps the Owens cooler than most streams, but by afternoon in midsummer it still warms into the mid-60s. When it reaches about 70°F, call it a day.

## Access

Much of the meadow is private ranch water, available to ranch guests. The public stretches are on Los Angeles Department of Water and Power land, reached from Owens River Road off 395 and from Benton Crossing Road. Park where it is obviously fine, close every gate behind you, and read the signs. Above Big Springs the water is called Deadman Creek and has its own rules.

In winter, the roads can need a high-clearance car or snowshoes. Call a Mammoth fly shop for conditions.

## The one thing

Approach. Most fish on the Owens are lost before the first cast. Walk slowly, kneel, and fish the near bank first.
